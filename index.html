I / Life Hushing

I thought I was hushing my house. I didn’t realize I was hushing my life.

☾ · ✦ · ❧

House hushing began with physical things: noticing what I was storing, organizing, cleaning around, moving, remembering, and maintaining simply because it was already there.

Then I started seeing the same pattern everywhere else. Identities. Work. Business. Reading. Rituals. Expectations. Imagined futures. Even the urge to turn every feeling into something I needed to understand, solve, or do something about.

Notice what has become loud See what I am maintaining Let what is finished be finished Stop rushing to replace it Live in the space that remains

Life hushing is removing the noise of assumed obligation until I can hear my own life again.

✦ A recognition
“Nothing was missing. There was simply less to do.”

Peace does not always come from finding a better practice, answer, identity, or way of living. Sometimes it comes from realizing that something I thought I had to do was never required.

II / The Field Notes

Notes from inside the life I am actually living. ✨

This is where I write what I’m seeing.

❧

These pieces may begin with a room, a feeling, motherhood, work, a book, an old rule, something I no longer want, or a moment when I realize I have been carrying more than I need to. They do not have to teach anything or turn into a method. They can simply be the living record of what I am noticing as life gets quieter.

III / About

I’m Megan. I tend to notice what does not reconcile, follow the thread underneath it, and keep going until something becomes clear.

For a long time, I assumed every meaningful discovery needed to become something: content, work, an offer, a practice, a better way to live. I am less interested in doing that now. I want this site to be a place where I can write from what is alive without requiring the writing to become anything else.

The writing is the premise. Connection is something that may happen because of it.

IV / Write back

The page stays quiet. You can still write back.

I do not want a public comment section here. I like that this space stays mine—without reactions, debates, or other voices collecting underneath the writing.

But I do love the possibility of correspondence. If something I write stirs something in you, you can send me a private note. No public performance. Just one person writing back to another.

✦

You do not need to have a polished response. A thought, a recognition, a question, or simply “this stayed with me” is enough.
