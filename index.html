I / Life Hushing

I thought I was hushing my house. I didn’t realize I was also learning how to stay here.

☾ · ✦ · ❧

House hushing began with physical things: noticing what I was storing, organizing, cleaning around, moving, remembering, and maintaining simply because it was already there. Then I began noticing that I could do the same thing internally—maintaining old explanations, old identities, old rules, and even the habit of turning every feeling into an investigation.

A note on the nameLife Hushing grew from Myquillyn Smith’s idea of House Hushing—quieting a home so it can breathe. As I practiced that in my own house, I began noticing the same question everywhere else in my life: What am I maintaining simply because it’s already here? Life Hushing is the name I’ve started using for what happened next.

Then I started seeing the same pattern everywhere else. Identities. Work. Business. Reading. Rituals. Expectations. Imagined futures. The urge to trace every feeling back to its origin. The belief that I had to understand what happened before I could move forward. Sometimes the quieter choice is simply to stay with what is here now.

Notice what is here Let the feeling be real Stop turning it into a problem Leave the past where it is Move from this moment

Life hushing is removing the noise of assumed obligation—and the noise of constant self-analysis—until I can hear what is actually true now.

✦ A recognition
“I stopped needing to figure myself out before I could keep living.”

Peace does not always come from finding the right explanation. Sometimes it comes from letting a feeling be a feeling, a preference be a preference, and a moment be a moment—without turning any of them into evidence that something is wrong.

III / About

I’m Megan. I notice patterns easily. For a long time, I followed every thread backward until I could explain what was happening. Lately I’m more interested in what happens when understanding is no longer a requirement for being here.

For a long time, I assumed every meaningful discovery needed to become something: content, work, an offer, a practice, a better way to live. I also assumed every difficult feeling needed to be understood. I am less interested in doing either now. I want this site to be a place where I can write from what is alive without requiring the writing—or myself—to become anything else.

Contact before explanation. The writing can begin here, without needing to resolve anything.

IV / Write back

The page stays quiet. You can still write back.

I do not want a public comment section here. I like that this space stays mine—without reactions, debates, or other voices collecting underneath the writing.

But I do love the possibility of correspondence. If something I write stirs something in you, you can send me a private note. No public performance. Just one person writing back to another.

✦

You do not need to have a polished response. A thought, a recognition, a question, or simply “this stayed with me” is enough.
