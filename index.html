The I AM View

What if the thing that feels true is only the interpretation you learned to make?

I’m interested in the ordinary assumptions that quietly become reality: “this is how it is,” “I have to wait,” “I don’t know how,” “they decide,” “I should fix this.” The work is noticing the hidden rule—and remembering that the current configuration is not the final word.

I / Start Here

I thought I needed better answers. What changed was seeing the assumption underneath the question.

☾ · ✦ · ❧

A desire becomes “I don’t have it yet.” A feeling becomes “I need to do something.” A current circumstance becomes “this is what I have to work with.” A person’s reaction becomes “this determines what is possible.” These moves are so ordinary they can disappear into the background and feel like facts.

The shiftNot replacing one rigid belief system with another. Seeing where an interpretation quietly became authority.

Once I can see the hidden rule, I do not have to fight it. I can notice what actually happened, what I made it mean, what I am treating as final—and choose again from the wider view I actually want to live from.

Notice what happened See the interpretation Name the hidden rule Remove false authority Choose from here

The interpretation may be familiar. It is not automatically truth.

II / The I AM View

A perception workbook for the thoughts that are so normal we forget they are interpretations.

✦ · ◌ · ☾

This is not a workbook for learning the “right” metaphysical beliefs. It is a mirror for the moment an ordinary thought becomes a rule you start living inside.

ExamplesCurrent configuration = reality.
Desire = absence.
Not visible now = future.
I don’t know how = limitation.
History = prophecy.
Feeling = instruction.
Discomfort = problem.
Outcome = verdict.

The practice is simple: What actually happened? What did I make it mean? What am I treating as authority? What am I assuming is final? What becomes available when I stop treating that interpretation as the truth?

III / About

I’m Megan. I notice patterns easily—especially the tiny rules hiding underneath the way we explain our lives to ourselves.

For a long time, I thought my job was to understand those rules well enough to manage myself better. Now I am more interested in the moment before that: seeing that the rule was never automatically truth. I keep finding that the current configuration can be real without being final, a feeling can be real without becoming an instruction, and another person’s experience can matter without becoming my authority.

See the hidden rule. Return authority to yourself. Let the rest be information.

Current conditionsI AM · perception · desire · choice · ordinary life · less management · more freedom

IV / Perception Audit

Bring the place that feels tangled. We can audit the perception underneath it.

✦ · ◌ · ☾

You do not need to arrive with a polished question or know what the conversation is supposed to become. You can bring the thing you keep circling, the desire that feels far away, the situation that seems to have authority over you, or the thought that feels so obviously true you have never thought to question it.

What happens hereWe talk. I listen closely. I notice the structure underneath what you are saying and reflect it back. Not so I can tell you what is true, but so you can see what you have been treating as true—and decide for yourself.

I am not interested in replacing your authority with mine. I am interested in helping make the hidden rule visible enough that you can hear your own view again.
