A few open questions

Finding the Space

You can trust what you're experiencing without having to decide that the way you're seeing it is the only way. Sometimes one gentle question changes the whole picture.

✦ Begin with curiosity, not correction.

I / Begin where you are

What's going on?

☾ · ✦ · ❧

You don't need to start with “Is that true?” If you've spent years questioning yourself, that may sound like one more reason not to trust what you experience. Start by hearing yourself.

A place to beginWhat's going on?
What's that like for you?
What feels important here?
What do you wish someone understood about this?

There is nothing you have to prove before your experience matters. Curiosity can arrive without withdrawing validation.

What's going on? What's that like for you? What matters here? What are you noticing?
✦ A little more room
“I can trust myself and still see something differently.”

Changing an interpretation does not erase the experience you had. It can simply mean you have room for more than one way of seeing.

II / When something feels fixed

What am I assuming has to be true?

✦ · ◌ · ☾

Sometimes two things both feel true, but we've quietly assumed they cannot coexist. The question isn't always which one to believe. It may be what makes them seem incompatible.

Self-trust“If I change my mind, I can't trust myself.”
What if trusting yourself includes the freedom to reconsider?
Relationships“For me to be right, they have to be wrong.”
Can you honor your experience while letting theirs belong to them?
Responsibility“If someone is disappointed, I have to fix it.”
Can you care without making their feelings your assignment?
Conditions“This practical obstacle means my need isn't valid.”
Can the constraint be real and your need matter at the same time?
Possibility“Because it's always been this way, it has to stay this way.”
What is familiar here, and what is actually required?
What feels true? What else is true? What makes them seem opposed? Does that have to be so? What opens up?

These are invitations, not steps you need to complete.

III / Experience and meaning

What happened—and what did I make it mean?

☾ · ✦ · ❧

Someone else's reaction can be real without becoming a verdict about you. A financial constraint can be real without invalidating your needs. A feeling can be real without assigning you the job of preventing it ever happening again.

A gentle distinctionExperience: What did I notice or feel?
Interpretation: What did I take it to mean?
Requirement: What did I then believe I had to do?

Sometimes that last question is where space appears. You can dislike something without making its disappearance a condition for enjoying your life. You can still make practical choices, set boundaries, and take real difficulties seriously.
