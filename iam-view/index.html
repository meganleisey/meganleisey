A perception workbook

The I AM View

Seeing the assumption underneath the ordinary interpretation.

✦ The point is recognition, not perfect thinking.

I / The practice

When something feels constricting, I look for the rule hiding underneath it.

☾ · ✦ · ❧

The thought that hooks us is often not the event itself. It is the interpretation that arrived so quickly and so normally that we never noticed it became a rule.

What actually happened? What did I make it mean? What am I treating as authority? What am I assuming is final? What do I choose?

I do not have to argue with the old interpretation. I only need to see that it was an interpretation.

✦ The wider view
“The current configuration is information, not authority.”

A configuration tells me what is arranged now. It does not tell me what must be arranged next.

II / Conditioned filters

The ordinary equations that can quietly become reality.

✦ · ◌ · ☾
Current configuration = realityConditioned view: “This is how it is, so this is what I have to work with.”

I AM view: This is what is arranged now. It is not the final word.
Desire = absenceConditioned view: “I want this, therefore I do not have it.”

I AM view: Desire can tell me what I choose without becoming evidence of separation.
Not visible now = futureConditioned view: “If I cannot see it now, it must be far away.”

I AM view: Not currently visible does not automatically mean distant.
I do not know how = limitationConditioned view: “I cannot see the path, so maybe it is not possible.”

I AM view: My current knowledge does not define the edge of possibility.
History = prophecyConditioned view: “This always happens.”

I AM view: History describes previous configurations. It does not dictate the next one.
Feeling = instructionConditioned view: “I feel this strongly, so I need to do something.”

I AM view: A feeling can be fully felt without becoming an order.
Discomfort = problemConditioned view: “This feels bad, so I need to fix it.”

I AM view: I do not have to solve an experience simply because I do not prefer it.
Outcome = verdictConditioned view: “If this goes badly, maybe I was wrong to trust myself.”

I AM view: An outcome can teach me without becoming the authority over whether I was allowed to choose.

III / The deepest check

What have I accidentally made the source?

☾ · ✦ · ❧

The current configuration. Another person’s decision. Money. Time. History. My present skill level. My body. A feeling. A role. A responsibility. An outcome. Visible evidence. The absence of evidence. The path I can currently imagine.

When I notice itI do not need to fight it.
I can simply recognize: I made this into authority.

Then I can ask again: What do I say? What do I choose? What do I know from the wider view?

IV / A note about this work

A mirror, not a doctrine.

I do not want this to become another system for policing thoughts or making yourself “better” at spirituality.

The workbook is useful only insofar as it helps make the hidden assumption visible. Eventually, you may notice the filters so quickly that you no longer need the workbook at all.

✦

The destination is not better thought management. It is recognizing that the ordinary interpretation was never automatically truth.
